exchanges on the mock WebSocket server, capture what the `:auth` namespace actually emits, and assert
the sequence a reader of the NIP-42 protocol would predict.

lines answer the question the requirements ask. The oracle must be derived from the protocol exchange,
not from the source — otherwise it is a change detector that would have passed before the phase started
(D-16, and the milestone's standing Verification Standard).

lifecycle, the two distinguishable failure modes, and concurrent attribution.

  <action>
Add the failure-mode tests. Each derives its expectation from what the scripted scenario physically does, not from the implementation's wording.

1. **Hung signer versus unresponsive relay** — the load-bearing pair D-09 exists for. Script two scenarios against the same auth-required refusal. In the first, the caller's signer never resolves, so the phase times out with no AUTH frame ever written to the socket. In the second, the signer resolves promptly and the AUTH frame is sent, but the relay never replies with `OK`, so the phase times out waiting. Assert that the first trace contains the signing line and no AUTH-sent line, and the second contains both the signing line and the AUTH-sent line but no result line. This is the concrete claim that the two silences are now distinguishable; without the signing line both traces would be identical.

2. **Relay rejects the AUTH** — the relay replies `OK` with `false` and a distinctive reason. Assert the result line reports a rejection and carries the relay's own message, and that the operation track's terminal outcome line is present. ALOG-01's "why it failed" is satisfied only if both the relay's words and the operation's outcome are readable.

3. **Retries exhausted** — configure a budget of one and have the relay refuse twice. Assert that the phase counter appears on the first phase's lines, that the second refusal produces the exhaustion outcome, and that the phase count named in that line matches the configured budget.

4. **Reconnect invalidation (D-12)** — after a successful authentication, drop the connection so `resetState` runs, and assert the invalidation line reports one dropped authenticated pubkey. Then, in a separate scenario, drop a connection that never authenticated and assert no invalidation line is captured. This is the line that makes the expected re-auth-per-reconnect cycle legible rather than appearing as an unexplained disconnect.

Record RED to GREEN non-vacuity probes for at least the hung-signer pair and the reconnect-invalidation pair: temporarily remove the signing line from `authenticate()` and confirm the first pair's assertions no longer distinguish the two scenarios; temporarily make the invalidation line unconditional and confirm the never-authenticated case fails. Restore the source both times, confirm green, and record all four observations in the SUMMARY. Restoring the source is mandatory — this plan modifies no production file in its final state.
  </action>
