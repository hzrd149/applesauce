<objective>
Close CR-02 and supply the REQ-side integration proofs for CR-01, CR-02 and WR-01.

**Structural choice (stated explicitly per planning directive 1):** CR-02 and CR-03 are one defect
class, not two. In both, a listen chain that (a) is `share()`d above the retry boundary and (b)
completes *inclusively* on the auth-required signal is re-subscribed synchronously from inside its own
terminal dispatch, before the share's reset has propagated. The uniform shape that removes the class —
the one 13-05 already established for `event()` — is: **nothing that completes on the auth-required
signal is hoisted above the retry boundary; the send side effect and the terminating listen chain are
both constructed per attempt inside a single unshared `defer`.** This plan applies that shape to
`req()`. Plan 13-10 applies the identical shape to `count()` and then audits the remaining sites
against the invariant, so the sweep is complete rather than incremental.

`req()` today has a second, distinct problem beyond `count()`'s: its REQ-sending `control` is nested
inside an outer `share()` (relay.ts:934) that also carries the listen path, so a synchronous resubscribe
rejoins the still-connected share and the REQ is never written to the socket at all — the verifier
observed 1 REQ frame, 1 handler call, a silent complete and 0 events.

CR-01 and WR-01 were fixed at their source in 13-08; this plan proves them on the wire. The REQ-side
bound test cannot be written before CR-02 is fixed, because the T-13-01 template
(`relay.test.ts:937`) drives its retries with a *synchronous* handler — exactly the case CR-02 drops.

Purpose: RAUTH-03 and RAUTH-07 are failed for `req`/`request`/`subscription` — 3 of the 8 operations —
and the defect propagates to every consumer of the read path through `RelayGroup` and `RelayPool`.
Output: a `req()` whose resend guarantee does not depend on share-reset timing, plus three wire-trace
tests that fail against today's code.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| relay WebSocket -> `Relay.req()` | A remote relay controls how many CLOSED auth-required replies it sends and whether it ever answers a REQ at all |
| `RelayGroup` / `RelayPool` / `SyncLoader` -> `Relay.req()`/`request()` | Every read-path consumer in the monorepo funnels through these two methods and inherits whatever guarantees they actually hold |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-13-09-01 | Denial of Service | `Relay.req()` retry loop | high | mitigate | Task 2's bound test pins REQ frames at `authRetries + 1` on the wire. Without it the 13-08 source fix is unproven on the path that actually propagates to `RelayGroup`, `RelayPool` and `SyncLoader`; the verifier observed 11 frames and 10 signer-prompt-equivalent handler invocations against one relay from a single caller intent |
| T-13-09-02 | Denial of Service | `Relay.request()` operation clock | medium | mitigate | Task 3 proves the clock fires against a silent relay. An unfireable clock lets a relay that accepts a REQ and then stalls pin a subscription and its buffers indefinitely, with no error surfaced to the application |
| T-13-09-03 | Repudiation | `req()` silent completion on a dropped resend | medium | mitigate | Task 1 plus Task 2's event assertion. Today a synchronous auth phase completes the subscriber with zero events and no error, so the application cannot tell "the relay has nothing" from "we silently gave up" — a wrong-but-plausible empty result rather than a reportable failure |
