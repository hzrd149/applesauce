<objective>
Extend the round-trip rule to the third lossy surface — the channel-edition fold — using
denylist-then-spread rather than a blind spread, and make `deleteChannel` preserve everything but
the addressing coordinate. Then prove, without changing it, that the community-metadata fold
already preserves unknown top-level keys.

Purpose: audit findings L02 and the channel half of L07. Phase 11's D-06 explicitly deferred the
channel-fold question to Phase 12. D-13 requires folds and editors to preserve what they do not
understand, and the current fold keeps only four fields and discards every other top-level key —
so a future protocol field on an edition is wiped by a client that does not know it yet.

But D-13 and D-14 collide here, and D-22 is the user's ruling on the collision. `tsc` stops OUR
code from reading an undeclared property; it does nothing to stop a hostile edition's raw JSON from
CONTAINING one. A blind spread in the fold would let a `MANAGE_CHANNELS` holder's `key` field
survive at the value level and round-trip back out through `deleteChannel`'s spread, reopening the
leak CHAN-04 closes today. The fold therefore destructures the key-material field names out
explicitly and spreads the rest.

Output: `ChannelMetadata` opened, the channel fold converted to denylist-then-spread with a rewritten
CHAN-04 comment, `deleteChannel` converted to destructure-and-spread, and three regression tests —
including the hostile-`key`-field test and the D-24 metadata-fold proof.
</objective>
