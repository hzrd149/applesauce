<objective>
Close WIRE-09 where it actually bites: the client publish tier. `ConcordClient` and
`ConcordInviteManager` each hold a reduced in-memory view (two arrays) and hand-roll the wire
document from it at publish time, so they structurally have nowhere to put a top-level key read
from the relay copy. Give each one a carrier for the document's other top-level keys, capture it on
read, and spread it back on save — then prove the round trip end to end through `ConcordClient`.

Purpose: this is D-23, and it is the difference between a phase whose headline guarantee is true
and one whose tests all pass while the shipped client stays lossy. Plan 12-07 fixed the parse layer
and the factory/operations layer, but `CommunityListFactory`, `modifyCommunityList`,
`InviteListFactory`, and `modifyInviteList` have zero call sites under `client/*.ts` — a round-trip
test driven through the factory chain never exercises `ConcordClient` at all.
`concord-audit.md`'s L07 already named both client sites; D-12's decision prose under-enumerated
them.

Output: a document-extras carrier in each of the two client engines, a mirror path that carries it,
and the phase's cross-cutting conformance suite.

Note on the carrier and D-12's rejection: D-12 rejected a bolted-on carrier field at the PARSE
layer, because there the root itself could be opened and the reconstruction deleted — a carrier
would have kept the reconstruction. At the CLIENT tier the situation is different: the engine's
state is genuinely a reduced projection (two merged arrays plus derived engines), the document is
not held in memory at all, and there is no reconstruction to delete because the arrays are the
authoritative merged state. A carrier is the structural fix here, not a patch around one. Record
this distinction in the code comment so a future reader does not cite D-12 to remove it.
</objective>
