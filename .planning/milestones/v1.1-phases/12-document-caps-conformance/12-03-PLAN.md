<objective>
Remove every serialized-byte cap on the invite side: `INVITE_LIST_MAX_BYTES` and
`inviteListWithinByteCap` with their single publish gate, and `INVITE_BUNDLE_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`
entirely — the constant declaration, its derivation doc block, the mint throw, and the validator
gate, all four sites (D-07, D-25). Rewrite every dependent comment so none cites a deleted
constant (D-10), and rewrite the four tests that currently assert the removed behavior rather than
deleting them (RESEARCH Pitfall 5).

Purpose: CORD-02 §8 and Appendix B derive their whole byte-ceiling rationale from a NIP-44
65,535-byte plaintext limit that no longer exists upstream. Per D-07, concord should not be the
thing inventing a ceiling upstream has lifted. D-09 establishes that the byte cap was never the
front-line guard against a hostile payload — the rule table and the count bounds are, and they
stay. This plan is the invite half; plan 12-05 is the Community List half.

Output: three source files with the byte caps gone and their justification comments rewritten,
and three test files whose assertions match the new behavior.

Requirement attribution: folded scope with no REQ-ID of its own, filed under WIRE-08 because
WIRE-08's requirement text is the only one referencing the byte cap ("enforces the 50-membership
protocol constant **alongside the already-enforced byte cap**") and D-07 is precisely what
retires "already-enforced byte cap" across all four constants.
</objective>
