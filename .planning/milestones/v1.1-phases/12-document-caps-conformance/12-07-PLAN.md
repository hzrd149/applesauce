<objective>
Make the two self-encrypted list documents' roots open, so the in-memory shape matches the wire
shape exactly and the write path can echo back what it read. `parseCommunityList` and
`parseInviteList` stop returning a closed two-field struct and stop renaming the wire's `entries`
key; mutation becomes shallow-copy-and-replace; serialization becomes a stringify of the whole
document (D-12).

Purpose: audit finding L07 and the governing principle D-01. Per-entry unknowns already survive,
because entries are modeled as pass-through data with an index signature — only the document root
is lossy, because the root is modeled as a closed struct. This plan makes the root behave the way
the entries already do. Critically, it deletes the reconstruction rather than adding a carrier for
what the reconstruction loses: D-12 explicitly rejects a bolted-on `unknown` record threaded
through parse → apply → stringify, because that keeps the reconstruction and relies on every future
write site remembering to spread it. The user challenged the premise of the parsed types
themselves; a plan that reintroduces a closed root has regressed this decision.

Output: two open-document parse layers, two rewritten operations, two re-pointed casts, a
re-pointed client mirror path, and round-trip tests at the helper and operations tier.

Scope boundary: this plan closes WIRE-09 at the helper, cast, and operations tier. The client
publish tier — `saveCommunityList` and `invite-manager.save()`, which hand-roll the document from
the client's own reduced arrays and have no field to carry a preserved key through — is plan 12-09
per D-23. Do not anticipate that work here beyond the minimal re-point needed to keep `tsc` clean.
</objective>
